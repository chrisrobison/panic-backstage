# Venue Safety

This is the full-depth safety document. For a fast, phone-readable version during an actual
emergency, use [Emergency Procedures](../staff/emergency.md) instead — come back here for
training and reference.

**This document does not substitute for a legally required Injury and Illness Prevention Program
(IIPP) or Workplace Violence Prevention Plan.** California requires both for employers.

## Exits and Occupancy

Every staff member should be able to name every usable exit from every space they work in,
without checking. Exits must remain unobstructed and unlocked in the direction of egress
whenever the venue is occupied.

Occupancy limits exist per space (the app has a per-venue-space capacity configuration concept),
but this document will not print a specific number without confirming it against current venue
configuration and permits.

## Fire

- Know the location of extinguishers and pull stations in your work area.
- Know your area's exits and a backup exit.
- If you smell smoke or see fire, treat it as real — don't wait for confirmation. Call 911.
- Only attempt to fight a fire with an extinguisher you've been trained on, and only if it's
  small and contained. Otherwise: get out, and get others out.

> **VERIFY — Confirm current Mabuhay Gardens procedure:** current extinguisher/pull-station
> locations, inspection schedule, and any staff fire training already delivered.

## Earthquake

- Drop, cover, hold on. Get under something sturdy and away from glass, hanging lighting, and
  speaker stacks/line arrays, which can fall.
- Stay down until shaking stops, then check for injuries and hazards before moving anyone.
- Do not use elevators after a significant quake until the building is checked.
- Assess visible structural damage before allowing guests back into any affected area.

---

## Medical Emergencies / Calling 911

- Call 911 for anything serious or ambiguous. It is always better to call and not need it than
  the reverse.
- Don't move an injured person unless leaving them in place is itself dangerous.
- Send someone to meet and guide EMS to the exact location — a music venue is disorienting for
  responders who don't work here every night.
- Every medical incident gets logged as an incident record afterward (see Incident Reporting
  below) — this is separate from, and does not replace, calling 911 in the moment.

## Active Threat

- Run if there's a viable path out. Hide if there isn't. Fight only as an absolute last resort.
- Never approach or attempt to negotiate with an armed or violent individual.
- Prioritize moving guests away from the threat over moving them toward the nearest exit if that
  exit is near the threat.
- Call 911 the moment it is safe to do so; follow law enforcement instructions fully on arrival,
  including keeping hands visible.

---

## Evacuation

- Direct guests to whichever exit is actually safe and closest given the situation — not
  reflexively the door they entered through.
- Move people with urgency but avoid creating a bottleneck or crush at any single exit.
- Once outside, get people moving away from the building to a clear assembly area, not
  clustering at the entrance.
- Nobody re-enters until a manager or emergency responder says it's safe.

> **VERIFY — Confirm current Mabuhay Gardens procedure:** designated assembly point(s) outside
> the building.

---

## Crowd Surge

- Recognize it early: people unable to move their arms, expressions of distress, pressure waves
  through a packed crowd.
- Stop admitting anyone else to that area immediately.
- Call for help (security/management); if anyone can't breathe or is going down, treat it as a
  medical emergency and call 911.
- If possible, open additional space/exits to relieve pressure — never add capacity to a
  packed area.

---

## Suspicious Package

- Don't touch, move, or open it.
- Clear people away from the immediate area.
- Notify the on-duty manager; call 911 if it genuinely appears suspicious rather than merely
  unattended (e.g., forgotten bag vs. something deliberately concealed or alarming).

---

## Power Loss

- Notify the on-duty manager immediately.
- Mid-show, the on-duty manager/production lead makes the call on pausing the show vs.
  evacuating — a dark room full of people is its own hazard.
- If emergency lighting doesn't cover the whole room, guide guests verbally toward known exits.

---

## Water Leak

- Notify the on-duty manager.
- Keep people clear of any area where water could contact electrical equipment or wiring.
- Contain what you safely can; don't attempt electrical work yourself.

---

## Broken Glass, Spills, and Slip Hazards

- Cordon or visibly mark the hazard immediately — don't leave it for the next person to find the
  hard way.
- Clean up promptly using appropriate tools (not bare hands for glass).
- Bar and floor staff should treat this as a standing responsibility during service, not just an
  incident response.

---

## Ladders

- Only staff trained/authorized to use a ladder for their role should do so.
- Never leave a ladder unattended in a guest-accessible area.
- Have a second person present (spotting/holding) when working at height where practical.

## Stage Safety

- Cable runs should be secured/taped to prevent trip hazards, especially across walkways.
- Rigging and hung equipment should only be handled by trained stagehand/production staff.
- Know the stage's edges and any trap doors, steps, or elevation changes, especially in low
  light.

---

## Electrical Safety

- No improvised wiring, no overloaded circuits, no water near open electrical connections.
- Report damaged cables, exposed wiring, or flickering/tripping circuits immediately rather than
  working around them.
- Only qualified personnel do electrical work — this is not a "figure it out" situation.

---

## Hearing Protection

- Sound levels at a music venue can cause real, permanent hearing damage. Hearing protection
  should be available to staff who work near the stage/PA for extended periods.

## Injury Reporting

Any injury — staff or guest — gets an incident record, tied to the event, using the app's
incident-record capability (`manage_incidents`; types include incident, damage, and others).
Incidents are restricted-visibility by design — that's intentional, not a bug, and it means the
record still needs to be created even though not everyone can see it afterward.

1. Handle the immediate situation (first aid, 911) first.
2. Get basic facts while they're fresh: what happened, who was involved, witnesses, time/location.
3. Log the incident record as soon as practical.
4. Notify the on-duty manager if you haven't already.
