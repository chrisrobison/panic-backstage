# Alcohol Service

This is policy — the rule and why it exists. The exact steps at the bar live in the
[Bartender SOP](../staff/sop/bartender.md); door-level ID checks are in the
[Door SOP](../staff/sop/door.md).

**LEGAL/REGULATORY REVIEW REQUIRED:** this document states general, well-established regulatory
context (below) but does not attempt to restate the full California ABC Act or local rules.
Anywhere this document describes an exact procedure, it should be checked against current ABC
requirements and Mabuhay's actual license conditions before being treated as final.

---

## The baseline rule

Nobody underage is served, ever, under any circumstance — no exceptions for "just one," a
regular's kid, a birthday, or a VIP request. This is the one rule on this list that isn't a
judgment call.

## Verifying age and acceptable ID

Staff serving or selling alcohol must verify age for any guest whose age isn't obviously and
unambiguously well past 21. When in doubt, card.

## Refusing service and intoxicated patrons

Staff may and should refuse service to anyone who appears intoxicated, and must not serve anyone
who is visibly intoxicated further, regardless of what they've already paid for or been given a
ticket/comp for. A guest who is cut off doesn't get served by a different bartender five minutes
later — this is a venue-wide decision once made, not a per-bartender one.

## Suspected drink tampering

If a guest reports or staff suspects a drink has been tampered with:

1. Stop the guest from drinking it further; don't dispose of the drink — preserve it if possible.
2. Get the guest somewhere safe, and involve medical attention/911 if there's any sign of adverse
   effect.
3. Notify security/management immediately.
4. Log an incident record.

## Incident escalation and documentation

Any alcohol-service incident — a cutoff that escalates, a fake-ID attempt, suspected tampering, an
intoxicated-guest injury — gets documented as an incident record per the app's incident capability,
the same as any other incident (see [Venue Safety](../staff/venue-safety.md)).

## RBS certification

California has required RBS (Responsible Beverage Service) certification for anyone who
serves or sells alcohol at an on-sale licensed premises since 2022. This is a real, current legal
requirement — not a venue preference — and applies to bartenders, and likely others who serve
alcohol depending on their role.
