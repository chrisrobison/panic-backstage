# Opening SOP

<!-- requires_acknowledgment: true — opening touches security systems, cash areas, and life-safety checks; default-safe. -->

## What this role owns

Whoever opens the building is responsible for confirming it's safe, secure, and ready before
anyone else — staff or guest — comes in for the night. This SOP is generic across roles; the
specific person doing it on a given night is named in Staffing or the event notes.

The opener must be a venue administrator, the assigned House Manager, or another staff member
whom venue administration has approved as a key/alarm holder. Having a key does not by itself
authorize someone to open for an event. The House Manager verifies the assignment before the
scheduled opening time.

## Arrival / Setup

1. Disarm the alarm and secure entry per current procedure. (This SOP will never state an actual
   code — see the Handbook's Keys/Codes policy.)
2. Walk the space before anyone else arrives: check exits are clear and unlocked in the direction
   of egress, check for anything left over from the previous night (spills, damage, unlocked
   doors), check restrooms are stocked and functional.
3. Confirm event details for the night against the system: event status, expected load-in times,
   staffing assigned (`manage_staffing`), guest list if applicable.

> **VERIFY — Confirm this venue's current procedure:** the exact opening walkthrough checklist
> currently used (if one exists outside this SOP), and whether the app's event record is the
> source of truth staff check against on arrival.

4. Confirm bar/cash areas are set up and opening drawer counts are signed per the Cash Handling
   SOP.
5. Confirm sound/production readiness with the sound/production lead for the night's event.

## During the Event

Opening staff typically transition into their assigned role's SOP once the building is open and
the event is underway (bartender, door, security, etc.).

## Before Leaving

Opening-shift-specific handoff: confirm whoever is running the event (House Manager function)
has what they need and knows the building is open and checked.

## When Something Goes Wrong

- Building doesn't look secure / signs of forced entry: do not enter alone — call the on-duty
  manager and consider calling 911 before proceeding.
- Alarm won't disarm or a system fault is shown: notify the on-duty manager immediately; don't
  guess at overrides.
- Any safety hazard found (see [Venue Safety](../venue-safety.md)): address if you safely can,
  otherwise flag it and keep the area closed off until it's resolved.

See also: [Venue Safety](../venue-safety.md), [Emergency Procedures](../emergency.md).
